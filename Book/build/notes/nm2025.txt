Background 
Electricity is essential for the socio-economic progress and for improving the living standard 
of the people. Bangladesh has already achieved 100% electrification. To ensure universal 
electricity access and energy security, the Power Division has adopted energy diversification 
as one of its key strategic approaches. Under this strategy, initiatives have been taken to 
generate environment-friendly electricity from renewable resources along with conventional 
fossil fuels. A significant increase in the use of renewable energy is also one of the core 
objectives of the UN Sustainable Development Goal 7 (SDG 7) . Besides, 20% of total 
electricity demand from renewable sources by 2030 and 30% by 2040 is set as target as per 
the direction of the Renewable Energy Policy, 2025 . 
 
The main sources of RE are – solar energy, wind energy, hydro, biogas, biomass, geothermal, 
wave and tidal energy. Due to its geographical location, solar is the most prospective 
renewable energy source in Bangladesh. A total of 1,350 MW of electricity generation 
capacity has been established so far by utilizing solar energy, majority of which come from 
solar parks, solar home systems, rooftop solar, and solar irrigation programs. But since more 
than 2 acres of land is required to generate 1 MW of electricity from solar energy, it has been 
difficult to arrange vast stretches of land to build large scale solar power plants.  Therefore, 
the prospect of generating electricity by installing solar systems on unused rooftops of 
various 
grid-connected 
facilities—such 
as 
residential 
buildings 
and 
industrial 
establishments—is being given serious consideration. Installing rooftop solar systems will 
significantly increase the contribution of renewable energy to overall power generation. 
Moreover, introducing incentive schemes for on-grid electricity consumers to install such 
systems will further encourage solar-based renewable energy production. 
To encourage renewable energy–based distributed generation, initiatives have been taken to 
introduce the net metering scheme. Under the net metering system, consumers use the 
electricity generated from renewable energy systems installed on their own premises and 
export the surplus electricity to the distribution grid. The exported electricity is credited 
against the consumer’s monthly electricity bill, and any remaining credit is adjusted in the 
following month. This process reduces electricity costs for consumers. Currently, over 50 
countries worldwide, including several SAARC member states, have adopted the net 
metering scheme. So far, in accordance with this guideline, more than 3,763 net-metered 
rooftop solar systems have been connected to the  six electricity distribution utilities’ network 
across the country, with a total combined capacity of 185 megawatts. As it reduces electricity 
expenses, consumers are likely to be encouraged to adopt the system. Under these 
circumstances, the Power Division has formulated and updated the “Net Metering Guidelines 
– 2025” to facilitate on-grid electricity consumers in availing net metering benefits through 
the installation of renewable energy systems. Based on implementation experience, the 
guidelines will be revised and refined as necessary in the future. 
1.1 
Objective of the Guideline 
 
This guideline has been formulated for the expansion of renewable energy and to utilize the 
electricity generated at consumer’s premises under the net metering system.

2.2 
What is Net Metering and How It Works? 
Net Energy Metering (NEM) is a system in which a prosumer or OPEX investor connects 
his/her solar or renewable energy system to the distribution grid. As a result, the consumer 
supplies excess electricity generated from his/her system to the national grid and 
subsequently draw electricity from the grid at the prescribed tariff rate. At the end of each 
billing cycle, the consumer is required to pay for the net imported electricity only. If the 
amount of exported electricity exceeds the imported amount, the surplus is carried forward as 
a credit. At the end of the settlement period, the consumer receives payment from the 
distribution utility for the credited electricity units. 
Under the Net Energy Metering (NEM) process, a bi-directional meter records electricity 
flow in both directions separately. This enables the measurement and adjustment between the 
electricity imported from the grid by the consumer and the electricity exported to the grid 
from the solar PV system or any other renewable energy source, in compliance with the 
provisions of this guideline. The import and export data are stored in the meter and 
transmitted to the central billing server of the distribution utility. For each billing period, the 
electricity bill is prepared based on the net electricity consumption, calculated by subtracting 
the exported electricity and the previously credited units from the total electricity imported 
from the grid, as described in the guideline. If the electricity supplied from the grid exceeds 
the total of the electricity exported from the renewable energy system and the previous credit, 
the consumer is required to pay only for the net imported electricity. Conversely, if the 
electricity exported from the renewable energy system and the previous credit exceed the 
electricity imported from the grid, the surplus is credited to the consumer’s account in 
kilowatt-hours and carried forward to the next billing period. In such a case, the consumer 
does not have to pay any energy charges for that month. 
At the end of the settlement period, the prosumer receives payment from the distribution 
utility for all the credits accumulated in kilowatt-hours, calculated at the rate specified in this 
guideline, after which the credit balance is reset to zero and begins a new cycle. The 
operation of an ideal Net Metering System is illustrated below through a PV system–based 
net metering setup:

In this context, the Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE) for electricity consumed by the 
customer depends on several factors, including the consumer’s tariff category, the type of 
renewable energy technology used, the installed capacity, and the limitations in power 
transmission. During the establishment of such an electrical interconnection, the prosumer 
must comply with all technical specifications and safety requirements prescribed by the 
competent authority to ensure safe and standardized interconnection. 
In accordance with this guideline, renewable energy systems under the Net Metering 
mechanism can be installed under two models — the CAPEX model and the OPEX model. 
Under the CAPEX model, the consumer installs a renewable energy system at their own 
investment and under their own management, thereby availing the full benefits of Net 
Metering. Conversely, the OPEX model refers to a system whereby a renewable energy 
facility is installed by a legally authorized third party under a tripartite agreement executed 
between the utility, the consumer, and the OPEX investor. The electricity generated from the 
system is supplied to the consumer as per the terms of that agreement. If the electricity 
consumer is unwilling or unable to install a solar system under the CAPEX model, they may, 
following due process, engage an OPEX developer company by determining the tariff and 
necessary conditions, and execute a bilateral agreement allowing the installation of the solar 
system under the OPEX model. Such bilateral agreement shall be referred to as an OPEX 
Agreement, which must be concluded prior to submission of the application to the 
distribution utility. Under this model, the electricity consumer bears no financial or 
operational risk; all installation, operation, and maintenance activities related to the solar 
system are undertaken by the OPEX investor company. The consumer purchases electricity 
generated from the solar system at the rate and under the conditions specified in the contract. 
The OPEX tariff is generally lower than the retail tariff of the distribution utility.

Net Metering Guidelines  
3.1 
Eligibility of Applicants  
An electricity consumer shall be considered eligible for Net Metering subject to fulfillment of 
the following conditions: 
 
A. He shall have to be a legitimate consumer of the distribution utility responsible for 
supply of electricity in the area concerned 
B. While submitting the application for a new connection, an applicant may also submit 
application for net metering in pursuance of this guideline; 
C. The applicant shall not have any outstanding arrears prior to submitting the 
application for net metering; 
D. Electricity to be produced ONLY from renewable energy sources would be brought 
under the scope of net metering;   
E. The applicant must either be the owner or have legal permission from the owner(s) or 
their legal representative(s) for installing the proposed renewable energy system in the 
premises of the owner.  
F. Roof of any building, parking lot, garage, commercial or industrial establishment or 
shed owned by the consumer or any empty space or boundary wall outside of the 
building but within the premises owned by the consumer or any other suitable area 
accepted by the Utility and where Utility meter exists is fit for installation of 
renewable energy system for net metering. 
G. The consumer shall consume the electricity generated by the renewable energy system 
and after consumption supply the remaining amount to the grid;  
H. Under the net metering scheme, in order to supply the electricity generated from 
renewables to the grid, connection shall be established at the existing electrical line in 
compliance with the interconnection rules and standards set by the Utility or any other 
existing authority;  
I. Instructions given in section 3.3 of this guideline shall have to be followed while 
determining capacity of the renewable energy system. 
J. Consumers who installed rooftop solar systems prior to the issuance of this Net 
Metering Guideline may apply to convert their existing systems into Net Metering 
systems. For such legacy systems (subject to proof of installation date through official 
utility or government documentation), compliance requirements for solar module 
standards may be relaxed;

3.3 
Capacity of Renewable Energy Systems and Limits on Export of 
Electrical Energy   
The maximum admissible capacity of the renewable energy system and the limit on export of 
electrical energy shall depend on the nature of the consumer and the pattern of use. Owing to 
technical constraints, the following initial conditions have been applied for determining 
system capacity and export limits: 
 
A. Any single-phase or three-phase consumer shall be deemed eligible for Net Metering 
subject to compliance with Clauses 3.1 and 3.2; 
 
B. The AC output of the renewable energy converter must not exceed the consumer’s 
sanctioned load; 
 
C. For medium to extra-high voltage consumers, the capacity of the renewable electricity 
generation system installed under Net Metering must not exceed 80% of the rated 
capacity of the transformer or the aggregate capacity of the parallel transformers; 
 
D. If a consumer intends to install a renewable energy system with capacity exceeding 
the contracted load limit, a separate application shall be submitted to the concerned 
distribution entity. If deemed appropriate, the distribution entity shall seek the opinion 
of the Grid Stability Committee (as referenced in Annex-9). Based on the 
Committee’s recommendation, the Power Division shall issue a decision. 
 
Based on experience gained from future deployment of Net Energy Metering systems, the 
Power Division, Ministry of Power, Energy and Mineral Resources may redetermine 
permissible capacities. 
 
3.4 
Energy Accounting and Settlement  
 
The following procedure shall be followed in case of energy accounting and its settlement: 
A. For each billing period, the concerned distribution utility shall prepare and send the 
electricity bill to the consumer in the format of Annex-7 (excluding pre-payment/smart 
meters). In case of pre-payment/smart meters, Annex-8 shall be followed. After 
adjustment under Clause 3.5, any credited units (kWh) remaining shall be carried 
forward to the next billing period; any net import shall be billed to the consumer. At the 
end of the settlement period (every three months), if any kWh credit remains, the utility

4.2.2 Voltage fluctuation 
Due to the inherent variability of renewable energy sources, the electricity generation from 
renewable systems under net metering may fluctuate. As a result, when electricity from 
renewable sources is supplied to the distribution network, it may cause voltage variations at 
the interconnection point and within other buses of the grid. 
The maximum allowable voltage fluctuation due to such variability shall be 6% for low, 
medium, and high voltage systems. If the fluctuation exceeds this limit, electrical equipment 
at the utility or consumer end may overheat. Therefore, an appropriate voltage regulation 
mechanism must be adopted to minimize such fluctuations when necessary.  
4.2.3 Power Factor of Electricity Generated from Renewable Energy 
The power factor is defined as the ratio of applied active power to apparent power. The 
inverter configuration of the renewable energy system shall be set (with the assistance of a 
Power Factor Improvement (PFI) unit, if required) so that the monthly average power factor 
recorded in the utility billing meter remains within the limits prescribed by the Commission 
for that consumer category. 
i. 
When the inverter operates at a load exceeding 20% of its rated output, the 
renewable energy system must be capable of operating at any leading or lagging 
power factor greater than 0.85. If a smart inverter is used, it shall automatically 
adjust to ensure that voltage rise due to power factor remains within allowable 
limits. 
ii. 
The required plant power factor shall be determined during the technical 
assessment phase.

4.2.4 Reactive power compensation 
For renewable electricity generation systems under net metering operating at unity power 
factor (pf = 1), all reactive power for the consumer load shall be imported from the utility, 
while real power will be a combination of on-site generation and imported utility power. 
Since self-generation typically requires a higher proportion of reactive power, the utility tariff 
meter may show a low power factor reading. To avoid this, consumers must comply with the 
instructions outlined in Section 4.2.3. In OPEX model solar systems, the required amount and 
conditions for reactive power to be drawn from the plant shall be explicitly included in the 
OPEX agreement.  
4.2.5 Injection of direct current 
The amount of DC current injected by the PV system into the Utility interface shall under any 
operating conditions never exceed 1% of the rated inverter output current. 
4.2.6 Harmonic 
The harmonic of a wave is a component frequency of a wave that is an integer multiple of the 
fundamental frequency. In the presence of non-linear loads (e.g., computer power supplies, 
electronic devices), alternating current may become distorted due to harmonic frequencies. 
Harmonics are measured as a percentage of the fundamental frequency or by calculating 
Total Harmonic Distortion (THD). Excessive harmonics can damage electrical systems and 
connected loads. To prevent such damage, the following measures must be ensured: 
i. 
The PV system output shall have low current-distortion levels so that other equipment 
connected to the Utility system is not adversely affected. 
ii. 
Total harmonic current distortion shall be less than 5% of the rated inverter output at 
the cable connected to the interconnection point. 
4.2.7 Voltage unbalance  
Voltage unbalance is defined as the ratio of negative sequence voltage component and 
positive sequence voltage component.  
i. 
Infrequent short duration peaks with a maximum value of 2% over 1-minute duration 
may be permitted for voltage unbalance.  
ii. 
When multiple single-phase PV units are installed, the unbalance shall be 
distributed evenly among the three phases of the power system. 
iii. 
The unbalanced voltage shall not exceed 1% more than 5 times within any 30-
minute period at the terminals of the consumer’s installation.  
4.2.8 Short circuit level 
The distribution utility must ensure that the short-circuit level of the network is appropriate in 
accordance with the equipment ratings. The maximum sub-transient three-phase symmetrical 
short-circuit current of the network must remain within 90% of the short-time make-and-
break capacity of the equipment. The distribution utility shall be responsible for verifying 
compliance with this requirement. Table 3 presents the standard equipment rating values

4.3 
Protection System 
The safety system of a renewable energy generation setup installed under the Net Metering 
framework must be designed in such a way that it can isolate any faulty section from the 
properly functioning part of the system when required. The prosumer shall design their 
protection system to ensure adequate safety based on their operational needs. However, Net 
Metering customers must comply with the protection requirements imposed by the utility, 
ensuring that any fault within the renewable energy generation system does not propagate 
beyond the system boundary. 
4.3.1 Smart inverter  
The network may become unstable due to voltage fluctuation at the interface of distribution 
network and RE resulting from increase or decrease of load at the user end. This may happen 
especially in case of high level of penetration of distributed generation. 
Advanced inverters, also known as smart inverters have a number of additional features along 
with the capability of converting direct current into alternating current. Smart inverters have 
the capability to assist in retaining grid stability when necessary. These features include: 
 Reactive power control 
 Active power control 
 Grid management 
The inverter used in a consumer’s system must comply with the requirements outlined in 
Sections 4.3.3 to 4.3.9 of this guideline.  
4.3.2 Frequency  
 
The distribution utility must maintain system frequency, and the PV system shall operate in 
synchronization with the utility’s frequency. The utility shall operate within a ±1% band of 
50 Hz, and the inverter must be capable of generating power within a ±4% frequency range. 
 
4.3.3 Synchronization 
Synchronization is the process of being coherent within allowable limits. The energy 
generation system under the net metering scheme shall have to be equipped with automatic 
synchronization system. In case of solar PV, synchronization has to be done at the inverter. 
 
 
 

===== p30 =====
28 
 
4.3.4 Anti-islanding inverter  
i. 
Anti-islanding or non-islanding inverters are unable to supply electricity in the absence 
of grid electricity. To ensure the safety of the solar PV system, it shall not be allowed to 
remain energized during outage of Utility grid (loss of mains). Within 2 seconds of 
power outage, the prosumer’s electricity generating system from the PV system shall 
have to be automatically disconnected, which is possible through an anti-islanding 
inverter. The grid-tied inverter used in the net metering system shall have to be capable 
of anti-islanding as per IEC 62116 (Utility interconnected photovoltaic inverters – Test 
procedure of islanding prevention).  
ii. 
Inverters used by the NEM consumer shall provide the following anti-islanding detection 
techniques:  
a. Under voltage 
b. Over voltage 
c. Under frequency 
d. Over frequency 
It shall also include at least one of the following active anti islanding techniques: 
a. Negative-sequence current injection,  
b. Impedance measurement,  
c. Slip mode frequency shift,  
d. Frequency bias, etc. 
iii. 
During the commissioning test, the NEM consumer shall have to prove whether the plant 
has anti- islanding capacity or not.  
4.3.5 Inverter fault current contribution:  
The inverter fault current is generally regulated by the inverter control. According to IEC 
61727 or IEEE 1547, the typical range of short circuit current is between 100% and 200% of 
the rated inverter current. The net metering consumer shall have to ensure that the inverter 
used by him meets the requirements of IEC 61727 or IEEE 1547. 
4.3.6 Protection schemes:  
The fundamental conditions for developing a protection scheme are as follows: 
i. 
No harm shall be allowed to the distribution network or any other consumer thereof 
due to any internal fault existing in the renewable energy system installed under the 
net metering scheme; 
ii. 
Adequate safety measures must be in place to prevent damage to the renewable 
system due to faults in the distribution network; 
iii. 
In addition to the above, the net metering consumer must maintain all supplementary 
safety measures necessary for both the distribution network and the renewable 
generation system.

4.3.7 Failure of system protection or control equipment:  
In the event of any system malfunction, the renewable energy system installed under the Net 
Metering scheme must be disconnected from the distribution network. The situations in 
which the renewable energy system installed under the net metering scheme must be 
disconnected from the distribution network are as follows: 
i. 
Failure of protection equipment 
ii. 
Failure of control equipment 
iii. 
Loss of control power  
 
4.3.8 Frequency Withstand Capability 
 
The renewable energy system installed under net metering must meet the Frequency 
Withstand Capability requirements specified in the Grid Code, for both under-frequency and 
over-frequency events.

4.4 
Safety Requirements 
 
The installation of renewable energy systems under the grid-connected Net Metering 
framework must adhere to both national and international safety standards. For the safe 
operation of a grid-connected system, due consideration must be given to the system topology 
and the earthing (grounding) arrangements. 
4.4.1 Operation  
i. 
In the interest of safety for operating personnel and the public, both the distribution 
utility and the Net Metering customer must maintain proper coordination. When any 
work or testing is carried out at the interface/connection point, necessary 
arrangements must be made to isolate the system from the distribution grid and to 
ensure proper earthing. 
ii. 
When any work or testing is performed at the interface between the distribution 
network and the renewable energy system—including rooftop solar systems under 
Net Metering—both the distribution utility and the Net Metering customer must 
strictly comply with all statutory laws, rules, regulations, license conditions, utility 
safety rules, and the existing Grid Code and Distribution Code of the country to 
ensure safety. 
4.4.2 Labeling 
Labels or markings on equipment and associated cables must be placed in clearly visible 
locations so that operators can easily identify them and exercise caution during operation or 
maintenance. This is essential because certain parts of the renewable electricity generation 
system may remain electrically energized.

13 Annex 8: Implementation of Net Metering on Pre-
payment/Smart Meters 
 
1. The pre-payment/smart meter and/or its server system must be made bidirectional, 
incorporating, in addition to existing parameters, the capability to record and store 
data required by net metering, including Export, Import, Credit, Settlement Unit, 
Settlement BDT Amount, etc. Historical data for Export, Import, Credit, Settlement 
Unit must be stored by settlement period. 
2. At the end of each settlement month (end of March, June, September, December), the 
accumulated credit units shall be treated as settlement units, and credit shall be reset 
to zero going forward. If settlement units are non-zero, the distribution utility shall 
provide the consumer a detailed billing document per Annex 7 and pay the value of 
settlement units via the specified method (bank/MFS/cheque). 
3. Each month, the distribution utility shall notify the consumer via SMS/email/mobile 
app/web portal and display on the meter: import, export, net export, previous credit, 
current credit, settlement units and their value. Total import and export readings must 
be recorded and displayed on the meter. 
4. At the end of each billing period, all relevant registers shall be updated per the 
Guideline after computing import units, export units, net export units, distribution 
system maintenance charge, prior credit units, etc. Any consumer credit units must be 
stored in the credit register. 
5. Against consumer usage, adjustment shall first be made from credit units; once credit 
reaches zero, deductions shall then occur from the recharged balance as per rules. No 
deduction may be made from the recharged balance while credit units remain. 
6. For slabbed consumers, slab calculation shall be based only on the units for which the 
recharged balance is deducted. 
7. Smart pre-paid meters used for net metering shall be re-programmable. If in future 
BERC applies a separate export tariff, the same meter must support separate tariffs for 
import and export. 
8. In all cases (pre-payment/post-paid/smart), the distribution utility shall share import 
and export data of each meter from the meter server with SREDA through an 
automated online system. SREDA may verify such data as required. 
9. New smart meters must incorporate the above provisions to enable activation by the 
utility as needed. For existing meters, the utility shall enable these features through re-
programming as required.
